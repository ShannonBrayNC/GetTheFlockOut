REVIEWED NOVEMBER 5, 2026 · ANGIER, NORTH CAROLINA

Anti-Flock petition:
rules, technical review & operating plan

Conclusion: use a community advocacy petition addressed to the Angier Board of Commissioners. Paper and online signatures can demonstrate support. Neither creates a verified right to an election, a hearing, a removal order, or a Board vote. This review found no general anti-Flock initiative authority in the published charter and state provisions examined. A claim to a compulsory remedy requires a separately confirmed legal basis.

Angier’s consolidated charter places policy in a four-member Board with a mayor who votes on ties. It uses a council-manager structure; special elections and referenda depend on general-law authority. It contains no broad voter initiative provision for ordinary policy. That supports the discretionary Board route, subject to checking later amendments and current town records. [1, 9]

Research limit: the enacted charter and legislative charter compilation were examined. The Municode charter endpoint did not yield readable text. A certified current charter, current adopted procedural rules and all later local amendments have not been obtained from the clerk. The conclusion is “no compulsory route established,” not a definitive legal opinion that no conceivable remedy exists.

2. Addresses, signatures and eligibility

For this advocacy campaign, name, city, ZIP, date and a self-reported town-residency declaration are a reasonable minimum. This is an organizer’s data-minimization choice, not a statement that a court or clerk has certified our form. No law requiring residential street addresses for this particular advocacy request was identified. City/ZIP cannot establish that a person lives inside municipal boundaries; an Angier mailing address can be outside town limits.

  • Town residents: report as self-reported residents unless separately verified against municipal boundaries with consent.
  • Other supporters: accept their support but report separately. Do not call them Angier voters or town residents.
  • No voter-registration claim: ChatGPT sign-in is an account control. It does not establish age, legal identity, eligibility to vote or one-human-only participation.
  • No street address, date of birth, ID or party data: do not collect these simply to make the petition look official.
  • Paper signatures: useful for access and outreach. Paper is not required merely because the petition asks for camera removal.
  • Meeting speakers: Angier’s November agenda instructs speakers to identify themselves by name and address and allows three minutes. That speaker instruction does not become a rule requiring every petition signer’s address. Confirm current instructions and any privacy accommodation with the clerk. [8]

3. Edited petition text and version control

Version 2026-11-05-v2

We ask the Angier Board of Commissioners to hold a public review and recorded vote on ending the town’s Flock program, ending or not renewing its Flock agreements as legally available, and removing town-controlled Flock cameras through an orderly process. While that decision is pending, freeze expansion and publish the contracts, costs, retention rules, sharing arrangements, and nonconfidential audit findings, subject to lawful exemptions. Preserve evidence and accountability records as required during any transition.

The revision identifies a requested decision without claiming an entitlement to it; covers termination and nonrenewal without assuming contract terms; limits removal to town control; acknowledges disclosure exemptions; and preserves required records. It does not claim that signing itself requires the Board to act. Use this exact text on each new paper sheet, website and notification. Do not attach old signatures to materially revised text. Earlier v1 records remain associated with v1; the mailer preserves the appropriate wording.

4. Paper collection and online notifications

  1. Print the full statement, version, disclosure, sheet identifier and signature fields on every sheet. Do not circulate an unlabeled continuation page.
  2. Let each person review the statement and enter their own support. Provide accessible assistance transparently; never fabricate a mark or imply a statutory election signature was collected.
  3. Explain that name, city, ZIP, residency declaration, date and signature are intended for submission to town officials and may become public records. Keep unattended sheets out of public view. Do not promise secrecy.
  4. Paper signing authorizes inclusion in the petition submission described on the sheet. It does not authorize an automated individual email, a mailing-list subscription or fundraising contact.
  5. Invite the signer to visit the site address printed on the sheet. A QR code can be added before public rollout. Online signing separately authorizes one notification per selected official and asks whether the person already signed on paper.
  6. Assign sheet/row identifiers. Maintain an internal reconciliation ledger. Use names, city/ZIP and the paper declaration as matching clues; resolve ambiguous matches with the person rather than merging different people. If using optional private contact data for matching, collect it separately with clear permission and exclude it from the town-facing sheet.
  7. Preserve the paper originals; maintain restricted scans and a record of receipt, counting, corrections and submission. The current app does not import paper signatures or automatically reconcile channels.

Counting rule: online submissions, paper entries, unique supporters, self-reported residents, and email notifications are different measures. Do not sum paper plus online into a unique-person claim until duplicates are reconciled. One supporter can authorize five notifications; that remains one supporter. Report unresolved counts by channel. A self-reported “already signed paper” checkbox helps review but is not proof of a matched paper record.

5. Submitting to Angier

Submit a cover letter, exact petition statement/version, reconciled summary, methodology and the signer records that people authorized for submission to the town clerk. Request a written receipt and distribution to the Board. Keep a copy of the submitted package. Ask for consideration at a regular meeting and identify a commissioner willing to request the item.

The March agenda packet’s proposed Rules 13 and 15 specify submission to the clerk at least two working days before a meeting and state that a public request does not guarantee an agenda item. Confirm the adopted rule and current deadline rather than treating a packet proposal as controlling. The November agenda publishes a three-minute comment period and an accommodation notice asking for 48 hours’ notice. Arrange access and presentation needs with the clerk early. [7, 8]

Ask for a written response or decision date as a request, not a guaranteed legal deadline. Do not promise that a petition will end a vendor agreement immediately. Obtain the executed agreements, renewals, notice deadlines, camera ownership and closeout duties before recommending a termination date. No December meeting date is certified by this review.

6. Flock-specific legal and factual accuracy

G.S. 20-183.31 requires a written ALPR policy before operation, including retention, sharing, training, supervision, access/security and at least annual audit/reporting; it limits handling to law-enforcement purposes, bars traffic-violation enforcement and requires maintenance/calibration records. These are oversight questions to investigate, not proof of an Angier violation. [10]

G.S. 20-183.32 generally limits captured plate retention to 90 days, with statutory preservation exceptions, and treats captured plate data as confidential. It does not require every sighting to be kept for 90 days. Seek contracts and segregable nonconfidential governance records, not confidential plate histories. Removal must respect applicable preservation duties. [11]

October 6 minutes included in November’s packet record a Flock policy discussion. Statements by public commenters are not independently established facts. The discussion does not prove the department lacked any prior written policy. The current adopted departmental policy, later amendments and executed contract remain needed. Do not allege a local hack, unlawful sharing, insider abuse, an identified driver or a security control’s absence without supporting evidence. [8]

8. Public-records and privacy plan

North Carolina’s public-records definition covers records received in public business. Treat the submitted petition and official emails as potentially disclosable, subject to applicable exemptions. Calling an email “private” does not make it exempt. The organizer’s account/email records are deliberately excluded from official notifications; a breach, legal process or later disclosure can still affect confidentiality. [12]

Before public launch, publish an organizer contact, controller identity, final retention schedule and correction/withdrawal process. Suggested organizer policy: retain working records through the Board’s decision and 90 days afterward, then review for deletion or a documented preservation need; this is a proposal, not an implemented timer or legal retention requirement. Keep contact/account fields out of public exports and social media. Do not submit or publish paper scans outside the consent given. Use separate explicit opt-in for any future newsletter.

9. Launch acceptance and verification

  • Configure ADMIN_EMAIL, verified MAIL_FROM, RESEND_API_KEY and ORGANIZER_CONTACT. Prove the admin can select the intended officials; show the same set to signers.
  • Verify sender-domain authentication and provider delivery/bounce handling using controlled test recipients. Do not send fake petition signatures to officials.
  • Test sign-in, successful storage, repeat-account/email rejection, malformed data, recipient/version changes, admin rejection, retry overlap and unknown provider outcomes.
  • Implement unattended retry/reconciliation with authenticated execution and alerting, or document a staffed delivery procedure before accepting public volume. Current retries are organizer-triggered; no scheduler was created.
  • Approve the retention and withdrawal procedure, accessible collection process and paper reconciliation ledger. Confirm current clerk submission instructions.
  • Open public access only after those checks. Public access is a separate configuration change and was not made during this review.

Verification performed: code and schema review; TypeScript checking; production build; mocked email-provider and submission-validation checks; SQL uniqueness/atomicity checks. These do not prove real email delivery, independent identity verification, voter eligibility or a compulsory legal remedy. See the accompanying source tests and operating README. No real signatures or messages are used in tests.

  3. Does any later charter amendment or local act establish a relevant initiative/referendum right? If so, obtain the exact authority before a separate formal drive.
  4. What executed Flock agreements, notice provisions, ownership and data-closeout duties govern ending the program?
